# Incoterms® 2020 — Cheat Sheet for Blockmediary
**Reference:** ICC Incoterms® 2020 (effective 1 January 2020)
**Relevance:** Incoterms define who bears cost and risk at each point in a shipment. For Blockmediary, they determine: (a) what documents the seller must produce, (b) when risk transfers from seller to buyer, and (c) what insurance is required — all of which feed directly into our document checklist and release conditions.

---

## What Incoterms Are

Incoterms® (International Commercial Terms) are ICC standard trade terms incorporated into international sale contracts. They define:
- **Where and when** risk passes from seller to buyer
- **Who pays** for freight, insurance, and customs duties
- **Which documents** the seller must provide

They do **not** define: title transfer, payment terms, governing law, or what happens if goods are damaged after risk passes. Those are governed by the sale contract.

**Blockmediary's role:** We verify that the seller has fulfilled their delivery obligation (as defined by the agreed Incoterm) by checking the required documents. The Incoterm used in the deal determines which transport documents and insurance certificates we should require.

## The 11 Incoterms® 2020 Rules

### Group 1 — Any Mode of Transport (7 rules)

---

#### EXW — Ex Works `(...named place)`
| | |
|--|--|
| **Risk transfers** | When goods are made available at seller's premises (or other named place) — not loaded |
| **Seller pays** | Nothing beyond making goods available |
| **Buyer pays** | Everything — export clearance, loading, carriage, insurance, import duties |
| **Insurance** | Buyer's responsibility entirely |
| **Key document** | None required from seller beyond making goods available |

> **Blockmediary use:** Least practical for our product — seller has almost no documentary obligations. Avoid unless the deal is domestic pickup. Not suitable for cross-border deals through our platform.

---

#### FCA — Free Carrier `(...named place)`
| | |
|--|--|
| **Risk transfers** | When seller delivers to carrier nominated by buyer at named place. If at seller's premises = risk transfers when loaded onto buyer's vehicle. If elsewhere = risk transfers when handed to carrier, unloaded from seller's vehicle |
| **Seller pays** | Export clearance + delivery to carrier at named place |
| **Buyer pays** | Main carriage, insurance, import duties |
| **Insurance** | Buyer's responsibility (seller has no obligation) |
| **Key documents** | Commercial invoice, packing list, export customs declaration, proof of delivery to carrier |

> **New in 2020:** Parties may agree that the buyer will instruct the carrier to issue an on-board bill of lading to the seller after loading. This solves the FCA + LC problem (banks need an on-board bill but under old FCA, the seller didn't have one).

> **Blockmediary use:** Common and practical. For FCA deals, require: commercial invoice, packing list, export customs docs, and carrier's proof of receipt. If parties want an on-board bill of lading, include that in the document checklist.

---

#### CPT — Carriage Paid To `(...named place of destination)`
| | |
|--|--|
| **Risk transfers** | When goods are delivered to the **first carrier** at the place of shipment — not at destination |
| **Seller pays** | Export clearance + main carriage to named destination |
| **Buyer pays** | Insurance, import duties, unloading at destination |
| **Insurance** | Buyer's responsibility (seller has no obligation) |
| **Key documents** | Commercial invoice, transport document (any mode), packing list, export customs docs |

> **Watch point:** Risk transfers early (at first carrier) even though seller pays freight all the way to destination. Buyers should note they bear risk during the main voyage despite not paying for it.

> **Blockmediary use:** Require transport document showing carriage contracted to named destination. No insurance certificate required from seller, but recommend buyer obtains their own.

---

#### CIP — Carriage and Insurance Paid To `(...named place of destination)`
| | |
|--|--|
| **Risk transfers** | Same as CPT — at first carrier at place of shipment |
| **Seller pays** | Export clearance + main carriage + **insurance** to named destination |
| **Buyer pays** | Import duties, unloading |
| **Insurance** | **Seller must obtain** — minimum ICC (A) cover (all-risks) under Incoterms 2020. This is an upgrade from 2010, which only required minimum ICC (C) cover for CIP. |
| **Key documents** | Commercial invoice, transport document, **insurance certificate/policy**, packing list, export customs docs |

> **New in 2020 (important change):** CIP now requires **ICC (A) / Institute Cargo Clauses (A)** — all-risks cover. This is more comprehensive than the old minimum (ICC C). Sellers must ensure their insurance policy meets this standard.

> **Blockmediary use:** Must require an insurance certificate/policy as part of the document checklist. Verify: coverage from shipment to named destination, minimum value 110% of invoice, ICC (A) or equivalent all-risks cover, no cover notes.

---

#### DAP — Delivered at Place `(...named place of destination)`
| | |
|--|--|
| **Risk transfers** | When goods are made available to the buyer on the arriving means of transport at the named place, **ready for unloading** |
| **Seller pays** | Everything up to the named destination, except import duties and unloading |
| **Buyer pays** | Import duties, unloading, local delivery from destination point |
| **Insurance** | Seller's responsibility during transit (no obligation to insure, but bears the risk) |
| **Key documents** | Commercial invoice, transport document showing delivery to destination, packing list, export customs docs |

> **Blockmediary use:** Seller bears risk all the way to destination. Our document checklist should include a transport document covering delivery to the named place. No insurance certificate required from seller (but they bear the risk if uninsured).

---

#### DPU — Delivered at Place Unloaded `(...named place of destination)` *(New in 2020)*
| | |
|--|--|
| **Risk transfers** | When goods are **unloaded** from the arriving means of transport at the named place |
| **Seller pays** | Everything including unloading at destination |
| **Buyer pays** | Import duties, onward delivery |
| **Insurance** | Seller's responsibility during transit and unloading |
| **Key documents** | Commercial invoice, transport document, packing list, export customs docs, delivery confirmation |

> **New in 2020:** Replaces DAT (Delivered at Terminal). The key change: DPU can be any named place, not just a terminal.

> **Watch point:** DPU is the only Incoterm where the seller is responsible for unloading. If the named place cannot handle unloading (e.g. a private warehouse), the seller is still responsible.

> **Blockmediary use:** Require proof of delivery and unloading. Consider a delivery/receipt confirmation document in the checklist.

---

#### DDP — Delivered Duty Paid `(...named place of destination)`
| | |
|--|--|
| **Risk transfers** | When goods are made available to the buyer, cleared for import, at the named destination |
| **Seller pays** | Everything — export clearance, main carriage, insurance, import duties and taxes |
| **Buyer pays** | Unloading only |
| **Insurance** | Seller's responsibility (bears all risk to destination) |
| **Key documents** | Commercial invoice, transport document, import customs clearance docs, duty payment evidence, packing list |

> **Watch point:** DDP places maximum burden on the seller. Sellers must be able to handle import clearance in the buyer's country — often not practical for SMEs unfamiliar with destination customs regimes.

> **Blockmediary use:** Document checklist must include import customs clearance confirmation. VAT/GST treatment at destination is a risk — our Trade Escrow Agreement should clarify who bears any unexpected import taxes.

### Group 2 — Sea and Inland Waterway Only (4 rules)

These four terms should **only** be used for sea or inland waterway shipments where the specific point of loading/discharge is identifiable at a port. They are **not** suitable for containerised cargo loaded at an inland point.

---

#### FAS — Free Alongside Ship `(...named port of shipment)`
| | |
|--|--|
| **Risk transfers** | When goods are placed alongside the ship (e.g. on the quay) at the named port |
| **Seller pays** | Delivery alongside ship + export clearance |
| **Buyer pays** | Loading, main carriage, insurance, import duties |
| **Insurance** | Buyer's responsibility |
| **Key documents** | Commercial invoice, packing list, export customs docs, delivery receipt alongside ship |

> **Use case:** Bulk cargo, oil, grain, raw materials — where goods are loaded by crane directly from the quayside.

---

#### FOB — Free on Board `(...named port of shipment)`
| | |
|--|--|
| **Risk transfers** | When goods are **on board** the ship at the named port of shipment |
| **Seller pays** | Export clearance + loading onto vessel |
| **Buyer pays** | Main carriage, insurance, import duties |
| **Insurance** | Buyer's responsibility |
| **Key documents** | Commercial invoice, **bill of lading** (on-board), packing list, export customs docs |

> **Most commonly used sea term.** However: FOB is technically not suited to containerised cargo (risk transfers at ship's rail, but containers are usually handed to the carrier at an inland container depot). FCA is more appropriate for containerised cargo — but FOB is widely used in practice regardless.

> **Blockmediary use:** FOB deals require an on-board bill of lading as the key transport document. Verify: "on board" notation with date, named vessel, port of loading = named FOB port.

---

#### CFR — Cost and Freight `(...named port of destination)`
| | |
|--|--|
| **Risk transfers** | When goods are on board the ship at the **port of shipment** |
| **Seller pays** | Export clearance + main sea freight to named destination port |
| **Buyer pays** | Insurance, import duties, unloading |
| **Insurance** | Buyer's responsibility (same risk/cost split as CIF but without insurance obligation) |
| **Key documents** | Commercial invoice, on-board bill of lading, packing list, export customs docs |

> **Watch point:** Like FOB, risk transfers at the port of loading — not at destination — even though the seller pays freight to destination. The buyer bears the risk during the sea voyage.

---

#### CIF — Cost, Insurance and Freight `(...named port of destination)`
| | |
|--|--|
| **Risk transfers** | When goods are on board the ship at the **port of shipment** |
| **Seller pays** | Export clearance + main sea freight + **insurance** to named destination port |
| **Buyer pays** | Import duties, unloading |
| **Insurance** | **Seller must obtain** — minimum ICC (C) cover (basic) under Incoterms 2020. Note: unlike CIP, CIF still only requires minimum ICC (C). |
| **Key documents** | Commercial invoice, on-board bill of lading, **insurance certificate/policy**, packing list, export customs docs |

> **Most commonly used LC term** for physical goods trade. Banks are very familiar with CIF document sets.

> **Blockmediary use:** Standard document set for CIF deals: invoice + on-board BoL + insurance certificate (min ICC C cover, min 110% of invoice) + packing list. Insurance certificate must show seller as insured, goods description, voyage, and claim payable to buyer or their order.

## What's New in Incoterms® 2020 vs 2010

| Change | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **DAT → DPU** | DAT (Delivered at Terminal) renamed to DPU (Delivered at Place Unloaded). Scope expanded — any named place, not just terminals. |
| **CIP insurance upgraded** | CIP now requires **ICC (A) all-risks** cover, up from ICC (C) minimum. CIF remains ICC (C) minimum. |
| **FCA + on-board BoL** | Parties may agree the buyer will instruct their carrier to issue an on-board BoL to the seller. Solves the FCA/LC mismatch. |
| **Bills of lading for FCA** | Explicit mechanism added so FCA can be used with LCs that require an on-board BoL. |
| **Own transport (FCA, DAP, DPU, DDP)** | Explicit acknowledgement that seller or buyer may use their own transport (no carrier needed). |
| **Security requirements** | Both seller and buyer now have explicit obligations regarding security-related requirements and clearances (e.g. pre-shipment inspection, ISPS). |

## Incoterms and Blockmediary Document Checklists

Use the Incoterm agreed in the deal to determine which documents to require:

| Incoterm | Must-Have Documents | Conditional |
|----------|-------------------|-------------|
| **EXW** | Invoice, packing list | Export customs (buyer handles) |
| **FCA** | Invoice, packing list, carrier receipt, export customs | On-board BoL (if LC-style deal) |
| **CPT / CIP** | Invoice, transport doc (any mode), packing list, export customs | Insurance cert (CIP only — ICC A) |
| **DAP / DPU / DDP** | Invoice, transport doc to destination, packing list, export customs | Import customs (DDP only) |
| **FAS** | Invoice, packing list, delivery receipt alongside ship, export customs | — |
| **FOB** | Invoice, **on-board BoL**, packing list, export customs | — |
| **CFR** | Invoice, **on-board BoL**, packing list, export customs | — |
| **CIF** | Invoice, **on-board BoL**, **insurance cert (ICC C, 110%)**, packing list, export customs | — |

## Common Mistakes to Watch For

| Mistake | Why It Matters for Blockmediary |
|---------|--------------------------------|
| Using FOB for containerised cargo | Risk transfers at ship's rail, but container may have been at the port for days. FCA is more appropriate — if seller insists on FOB, ensure the BoL date reflects actual loading. |
| CIF with a cover note instead of insurance policy | UCP 600 Art 28(c) prohibits cover notes. Reject and request an insurance policy or certificate. |
| Wrong Incoterm for the transport mode | FAS, FOB, CFR, CIF cannot be used for air or road shipments. If an air shipment is described as "CIF (airport)" — the term is technically wrong, but assess the intent and document set practically. |
| DDP without import customs docs | If the deal is DDP, seller must provide import clearance evidence. Missing import docs = incomplete presentation. |
| "CIF value" discrepancy in insurance | Insurance must be at least 110% of CIF value. If seller provides a policy for exactly the invoice value, it may be short. |

## Dispute Scenarios Relevant to Blockmediary

**Scenario 1:** Goods shipped FOB, on-board BoL presented. Buyer claims goods were damaged at sea and refuses to confirm receipt.
> **Answer:** Under FOB, risk transferred when goods were on board the ship. The seller fulfilled their obligation. If the on-board BoL is presented (clean, on-board notation, correct port), Blockmediary should release funds — the buyer's dispute about damage is a matter for their cargo insurer, not for the escrow.

**Scenario 2:** CIF deal. Seller submits an insurance cover note, not a policy.
> **Answer:** Cover notes are explicitly not acceptable under UCP 600 Art 28(c). Hold for documentary review and request the insurance policy or certificate.

**Scenario 3:** CPT deal. Buyer says risk didn't transfer because the goods never arrived.
> **Answer:** Under CPT, risk transferred at the first carrier. If the seller's transport document shows handover to carrier, the seller has fulfilled their obligation. Release funds per the document checklist — the buyer's claim against the carrier or insurer is separate.
