# UCP 600 — Cheat Sheet for Blockmediary
**Reference:** ICC Publication No. 600 (2007) — Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits
**Relevance:** Blockmediary's documentary release logic is UCP 600-inspired. This sheet covers the articles that directly govern how our document verification and fund release rules should be designed.

---

## What UCP 600 Is

UCP 600 is the ICC's rulebook for **Letters of Credit (LCs)**. It governs how banks issue, examine, and honour documentary credits worldwide. It is incorporated by reference into an LC — it has no force of law on its own.

**Blockmediary is not issuing an LC.** Our Trade Escrow Agreement is a separate instrument. However, we model our documentary release logic on UCP 600 principles because:
1. It is the globally accepted standard for documentary trade.
2. Courts and trade professionals understand it.
3. "UCP 600-inspired" gives our release rules credibility and a clear reference point.

**Always say:** Blockmediary provides *UCP 600-inspired* documentary release logic — not a letter of credit.

## Key Definitions (Article 2)

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Complying presentation** | A presentation of documents that accords with the terms and conditions of the credit, the applicable provisions of UCP 600, and standard banking practice |
| **Credit** | Any arrangement that is irrevocable and constitutes a definite undertaking by the issuing bank to honour a complying presentation |
| **Documents** | Anything required by the credit — invoice, transport doc, insurance, certificates, etc. |
| **Honour** | Pay at sight, incur a deferred payment undertaking, or accept and pay a bill of exchange |
| **Issuing bank** | The bank that issues the credit at the applicant's request |
| **Nominated bank** | The bank authorised to pay, incur deferred payment, accept, or negotiate |
| **Presenter** | The beneficiary or any other party making a presentation of documents |

## The Autonomy Principle (Articles 4 & 5)

**Article 4 — Credits vs. Contracts:**
A credit is independent of the underlying sale contract. Banks deal only in documents, not in goods, services, or performance. Even if the buyer claims the goods are defective, the bank must pay if documents comply.

> **For Blockmediary:** Our escrow is also independent of the underlying trade. If documents presented to us are compliant with the agreed checklist, we release funds — we do not investigate whether the goods actually arrived in good condition. This is the key feature that makes us an LC *alternative*, not just an escrow service.

**Article 5 — Documents, Not Goods:**
Banks examine documents, not the physical goods. Compliance is assessed on the face of the documents only.

> **For Blockmediary:** Same principle — our AI document verification checks what is written on the document, not what is in the shipment. This must be clearly disclosed to parties in our Trade Escrow Agreement.

## Standard for Examination of Documents (Article 14) — CRITICAL

This is the most operationally important article for Blockmediary's document verification engine.

### Article 14(a) — The 5-Banking-Day Rule
A nominated bank, confirming bank, or issuing bank has **a maximum of 5 banking days** following the day of presentation to examine and decide whether to honour or refuse. Our equivalent: the document review window.

### Article 14(b) — Not Required to Match Each Other Word for Word
Data in a document need not be identical to the credit or other documents, but **must not conflict** with data in that document, any other stipulated document, or the credit.

> **For Blockmediary:** Our AI checker should flag *conflicts* between documents, not just variations in phrasing. e.g. "5,000 metric tons" on the invoice vs "5 MT" on the bill of lading should flag for human review.

### Article 14(c) — Transport Document Date = Shipment Date
If a credit requires a transport document, it must be presented within 21 calendar days of the date of shipment. **Our equivalent:** build a shipment-date-to-presentation-deadline check.

### Article 14(d) — Description of Goods
The description of goods in the commercial invoice must correspond to the credit. In all other documents, the goods may be described in general terms not inconsistent with the credit.

### Article 14(e) — Shipper / Consignee
In transport documents, the shipper or consignor need not be the beneficiary. Banks will accept transport documents showing any party as shipper.

### Article 14(f) — Multiple Originals
If a credit requires multiple originals of a document (e.g. "3/3 original bills of lading"), all must be presented.

### Article 14(g) — Conditions Without Documents
If a credit contains a condition without specifying a document to evidence compliance, banks will disregard the condition. **Lesson for Blockmediary:** every compliance condition we set must be tied to a specific document we will examine.

### Article 14(h) — Non-Documentary Conditions
Banks disregard non-documentary conditions — they cannot check compliance unless a document evidences it.

### Article 14(j) — Addresses
Addresses of buyer and seller need not match across documents exactly, provided they are in the same country as stated in the credit. Contact details are disregarded.

## Complying Presentation (Article 15)

Once a bank determines a presentation complies:
- **Issuing bank:** must honour.
- **Confirming bank:** must honour or negotiate and forward docs to issuing bank.
- **Nominated bank (having honoured):** must forward docs.

> **For Blockmediary:** Once our document verification confirms compliance, the smart contract **must** trigger fund release. There is no discretion to delay or refuse once the compliance check passes — this is the key trust guarantee to the seller.

## Discrepant Documents, Waiver, Notice (Article 16)

### Refusal
If a bank determines the presentation does not comply, it **may** refuse to honour. Refusal requires a formal notice stating **each discrepancy**.

### Notice Requirements (Article 16(c) & (d))
- The notice must state each discrepancy relied upon.
- It must be given **no later than the close of business on the 5th banking day** after presentation.
- If no notice is given within 5 days, the bank is **precluded** from claiming the documents are discrepant.

> **For Blockmediary:** If our system does not complete the document check and issue a rejection notice within our review window, we cannot later claim documents were non-compliant. Build a hard deadline into the workflow — missing the window = funds release.

### Waiver
The issuing bank may approach the applicant for a waiver of discrepancies. The applicant's waiver does not bind the bank to honour — but in practice it usually leads to payment.

> **For Blockmediary:** In our model, the "applicant" (buyer) can waive a discrepancy and agree to release funds even if documents are technically non-compliant. Build a waiver mechanism into the platform.

### Options on Refusal (Article 16(c)(iii))
On refusal, the bank may:
- Hold the documents pending applicant waiver instructions, or
- Return the documents to the presenter, or
- Act on previously given instructions.

## Original Documents (Article 17)

A document is original if it bears an **apparently original signature, mark, stamp, or label** of the issuing party. At least **one original** of each required document must be presented.

Banks may accept an original even where only a copy is stipulated.

> **For Blockmediary:** Our document upload process should collect at least one original (or certified copy) of each required document. For electronic trade documents (eDocs), reference the **eUCP** supplement and ensure the electronic equivalent of "original" is clearly defined.

### Electronic Trade Documents — UK ETDA 2023

The **UK Electronic Trade Documents Act 2023 (ETDA)** gives electronic bills of lading the same legal status as paper originals under English law, but only if the **"control" requirement** is met: only one person at a time can hold, use, and transfer the document (singularity of control). A PDF emailed to multiple parties does **not** satisfy this.

| Document type | Blockmediary action |
|---|---|
| Scanned paper document (PDF of signed original) | Accept — ETDA does not apply to paper |
| eBL from a qualifying system (BOLERO, essDOCS, WaveBL) | Accept — require presenter to confirm control transfer |
| PDF copy of a BoL emailed to parties | **Do not** treat as original — request scanned paper original or qualifying eBL |

**MVP policy:** Accept scanned PDFs only. eBL integration is post-MVP. Equivalent laws: DIFC Electronic Commerce Law (UAE/DIFC), Singapore ETA 2010. See [docs/legal-risk.md §1.8](legal-risk.md).

## Commercial Invoice (Article 18)

- Must be issued by the **beneficiary** (seller).
- Must be made out in the **name of the applicant** (buyer).
- Must be in the **same currency** as the credit — avoids FX ambiguity.
- The description of goods in the invoice must **match the credit exactly**.

> **For Blockmediary:** Our invoice checklist must verify: issuer = seller, consignee = buyer, currency = USDC equivalent or agreed fiat, goods description matches the Deal Agreement. Currency match is especially important for stablecoin-denominated deals.

## Transport Documents (Articles 19–24)

### Which Document for Which Mode?

| Article | Document | Mode |
|---------|----------|------|
| Art. 19 | Multimodal / combined transport document | Any combination of modes |
| Art. 20 | **Bill of Lading** | Sea / inland waterway |
| Art. 21 | Non-negotiable Sea Waybill | Sea (no title transfer) |
| Art. 22 | Charter Party Bill of Lading | Chartered vessel |
| Art. 23 | Air Transport Document (Air Waybill) | Air |
| Art. 24 | Road, Rail, or Inland Waterway Transport Document | Land / river |

### Key Rules for Bills of Lading (Article 20)
- Must show the **port of loading and port of discharge** as in the credit.
- Must indicate goods have been **shipped on board** (or have an on-board notation with date).
- Must be signed by: carrier, master, agent for carrier, or agent for master.
- Transhipment: a bill of lading indicating transhipment **must** be accepted even if the credit prohibits it, **provided** the whole voyage is covered under one bill and the goods are in a container/trailer/LASH barge.

### "On Board" Notation
Requires that the transport document states the goods were loaded on board a named vessel. If the bill of lading doesn't say "on board," an on-board notation signed/stamped by the carrier with the shipment date is required.

> **For Blockmediary:** Build transport document type detection into our doc checker. For sea shipments, verify: "on board" notation, date of shipment, named vessel, port of loading/discharge. For air waybills, the date of issue = date of shipment unless a flight date notation is present.

## Clean Transport Document (Article 27)

Banks will **only accept** a clean transport document — one that does **not** bear clauses or notations indicating a defect in the condition of goods or packaging.

The word "clean" need not appear on the transport document even if the credit requires it to be "clean on board."

> **For Blockmediary:** Our document review must check for any clauses noting damaged goods, short shipment, or condition issues. Any such clause = automatic hold for human review.

---

## Insurance (Article 28)

- Insurance document must show that **coverage is effective from the place of taking in charge or shipment** through to the place of final destination.
- Must be for at least **110% of the CIF or CIP value** (or credit amount if CIF/CIP not stated).
- Cover notes are **not acceptable** — only insurance policies or certificates.
- The insured party on the document must be able to make a claim (usually the buyer or "to order").

> **For Blockmediary:** If insurance is a required document (typically for CIF/CIP Incoterms deals), check: coverage period, minimum value (110% of invoice), and that cover notes are not submitted.

## Time Limits (Articles 29 & 30)

**Article 29 — Expiry Extension:**
If expiry falls on a non-banking day, it extends to the next banking day. Does not extend the shipment deadline.

**Article 30 — Tolerance:**
- Credit amount, quantity, unit price: ±10% tolerance only if the word "about" or "approximately" is used.
- Quantity of goods (not expressed in units): ±5% tolerance even without the word "about."
- Amount drawn: must not exceed the credit amount.

> **For Blockmediary:** Build tolerance checks into the invoice/quantity verification. If the deal says "approximately 100 MT," accept 90–110 MT. If it says "100 MT" exactly, hold for discrepancy if the quantity differs.

## eUCP — Electronic Presentation Supplement

The **eUCP** (Electronic UCP) is a supplement to UCP 600 that accommodates electronic documents in place of paper. Key points:
- An electronic record must appear on its face to fulfil the function of a paper document.
- The presenter warrants that the electronic record is authentic.
- A nominated bank or issuing bank can reject an electronic record that cannot be authenticated.

> **For Blockmediary:** As we accept digital document uploads, we should reference eUCP principles. Our document upload terms should include a representation by the presenter that uploaded documents are authentic and have not been altered.

---

## ICC DOCDEX — Documentary Dispute Resolution

**DOCDEX** (Documentary Instruments Dispute Resolution Expertise) is the ICC's fast-track expert opinion service for documentary credit disputes. A panel of three ICC-accredited experts delivers a decision within 30 days.

- Not arbitration — the decision is binding only if both parties agree in advance.
- Cost: lower than arbitration; suitable for documentary disputes (discrepancy claims, wrongful dishonour).
- Decisions available in English, French, or Spanish.

> **For Blockmediary:** Include ICC DOCDEX as the **first-line dispute resolution mechanism** in our Trade Escrow Agreement for documentary disputes. For larger commercial disputes (e.g. fraud, contract breach beyond documents), escalate to LCIA or ICC arbitration.

## UCP 600 Quick-Reference: What Blockmediary Must Check

| Document | Key Checks |
|----------|-----------|
| **Commercial Invoice** | Issued by seller; consignee = buyer; currency matches deal; goods description matches exactly |
| **Bill of Lading** | "On board" notation + date; named vessel; port of loading/discharge match; clean (no damage clauses); correct originals count |
| **Air Waybill** | Date of issue = shipment date (or flight notation); consignee = buyer or carrier's agent; airport codes match |
| **Insurance Certificate** | Coverage from shipment to destination; min 110% of invoice value; no cover note |
| **Certificate of Origin** | Issued by correct authority; country matches corridor |
| **Packing List** | Quantities and descriptions consistent with invoice |
| **Inspection Certificate** | Issued by named inspector; date before shipment; findings consistent with goods |
