# Presenter Script — Slides 4, 5, 6

**Transakt — Blockmediary** · 40 seconds · deck: `business-model-canvas.pptx`

Covers Product Architecture (04), Market (05) and Business Model Canvas (06).
**102 words ≈ 40–41 seconds** at pitch pace (~150 wpm).

Register: formal pitch delivery. Complete sentences throughout; transitions state the
relationship between slides rather than dramatise it. No rhetorical fragments.

---

## The script

### Slide 4 — Product Architecture · 0:00–0:11

> Three tiers separate concerns: the escrow contract holds funds and enforces state, but
> never sees a document. Verification is performed off-chain, bridged by one authorised key.

### → TRANSITION · 0:11–0:13

> **That architecture serves one segment.**

### Slide 5 — Market · 0:13–0:25

> Our launch corridor, UAE–India, moves 65 billion dollars a year. We target consignments
> of ten to fifty thousand dollars — below what banks will underwrite, above what firms
> will trade on trust.

### → TRANSITION · 0:25–0:28

> **The commercial model follows from that positioning.**

### Slide 6 — Business Model Canvas · 0:28–0:41

> Customers choose their verification tier, pricing their own cost of trust. As electronic
> bills of lading spread, that blend falls toward one percent. Year three returns 1.37
> million pounds on two thousand deals. We address documentary trust, not working-capital
> finance.

---

## Why slide 6 reads this way

The canvas already displays the fee tiers, the revenue line and the cost base. Narration
that recites them adds nothing an assessor cannot read faster than you can speak. The
eleven seconds are better spent on the one proposition the slide cannot make on its own:

1. **The mechanism is customer-controlled.** "Customers choose their verification tier,
   pricing their own cost of trust" states what the tiered fee actually *is* — a
   self-selection mechanism, not a price list. This is the genuinely distinctive design
   choice on the canvas.
2. **The economics improve structurally.** As eBL adoption grows, the mix shifts to the
   cheap tier and the blended take rate falls 2.0% → 1.0% while gross margin holds at
   71–79%. The product gets cheaper for customers *and* stays profitable, because
   verification cost falls with market digitisation. That is a defensible moat claim and
   it was absent from earlier drafts.
3. **One anchor figure, not four.** £1.37m on 2,000 deals is enough to establish scale.
   Break-even, margin and funding are on the slide for anyone who wants them.
4. **The boundary closes the sequence.** It is the line that separates a credible
   compliance posture from an overclaim, and it belongs last, where it lands.

## Transitions

Both transitions are subordinating rather than declarative — each states how the next slide
depends on the one before it, which is what carries an argument forward in a pitch.

| Transition | Function |
|---|---|
| *"That architecture serves one segment."* | Moves from build to market by asserting the architecture was designed **for** that segment, not adapted to it. |
| *"The commercial model follows from that positioning."* | Frames the canvas as a **consequence** of the market choice, so the figures read as derived rather than asserted. |

**Alternatives, same register:**

- 4 → 5: *"That design is built for a specific segment of cross-border trade."* ·
  *"Turning to the market that architecture is built to serve."*
- 5 → 6: *"That positioning determines the commercial model."* ·
  *"The economics of that segment are as follows."*

**If you have 45 seconds rather than 40**, restore the two figures trimmed for time:
open slide 5 with *"The UAE processed 1.03 trillion dollars in non-oil trade last year,
of which our corridor accounts for 65 billion"*, and append *"with cash-flow break-even in
year four"* to slide 6. That adds roughly 5 seconds.

## Delivery notes

- Deliver each transition **on** the slide change, at the same pace as the body text.
  A transition spoken faster than the surrounding sentences reads as an aside.
- Mark a beat after *"never sees a document"* (slide 4) — it is the strongest single claim
  in the architecture and needs room to register.
- On slide 6, the sentence that carries the argument is *"that blend falls toward one
  percent."* Do not throw it away as a subordinate clause; it is the moat.
- Mark a beat before *"We address documentary trust"*. That clause is the one a
  compliance-minded assessor is listening for; it should not arrive at speed.
- Three slides in 40 seconds allows roughly 13 seconds each. One proposition per slide.
  Adding a second clause anywhere will overrun.

## Spoken numbers

*"one point three seven million"* · *"two thousand"*.
