The main constraint is not the age of the business. It is the unresolved regulatory perimeter: whether 
Blockmediary’s smart-contract and server-side release controls amount to custody, transfer and settlement, 
or a technology service supporting a licensed principal. The answer determines entity structure, capital, 
fees, safeguarding and the design of the pilot.
2. Blockmediary’s regulatory boundary
The Blockmediary BRD defines a prefunded documentary escrow. A buyer sends stablecoin directly to a 
smart contract; Blockmediary verifies trade documents off-chain; an authorised server-side function 
submits the release decision on-chain. Blockmediary does not lend, provide working capital, guarantee the 
goods, or route customer funds through an ordinary Blockmediary wallet. The delivered prototype is 
testnet-based and uses a deterministic document-rules engine. Production KYB/sanctions screening, legal 
agreements, human-review operations and partner API hardening remain roadmap items. [1]

Important interpretation
“The smart contract holds the assets” is a technical description, not a complete regulatory conclusion. 
Regulators generally examine who can cause, prevent, reverse, upgrade or recover transfers and who 
bears responsibility to the client. Blockmediary should not describe itself as definitively non-custodial until 
that control analysis is documented.
3. Track A — DIFC partner-led pilot
3.1 What the route is
A “DIFC partner-led pilot” is not a named DFSA licence category. It is a proposed operating arrangement in 
which a DFSA-authorised firm acts as the regulated principal and Blockmediary supplies technology, 
document-verification and workflow services. Firms conducting financial services in or from DIFC require 
DFSA authorisation, and a DIFC Commercial or Innovation Licence does not itself permit regulated financial 
services. [3][4][5]

3.3 Recommended pilot envelope
For this preliminary business plan, the proposed pilot is deliberately controlled rather than fully scaled:
 Business-to-business customers only, with a small named cohort recruited through the regulated partner.
 Initial deal values at or below the current £50,000 automation cap, with human sign-off available for every 
release.
 Approved low-risk corridors and non-prohibited goods; no sanctioned parties or high-risk regulated goods.

 A narrow document set and explicit release rules; no claim that the platform verifies physical quality or 
delivery.
 Partner-approved token, blockchain, wallets and funding/redemption path.
 Manual exception, complaint, refund and incident procedures before the first live transaction.
 No public claim that Blockmediary itself is DFSA-authorised unless and until it obtains a DFSA licence.
3.4 Is a DFSA Innovation Testing Licence required?
Not automatically. The ITL is a restricted DFSA sandbox for a firm that itself needs to test regulated financial 
services. A genuinely partner-led arrangement may instead operate within the authorised partner’s 
permissions and outsourcing framework. Blockmediary should pursue an ITL only if the DFSA or counsel 
concludes that Blockmediary itself will conduct a regulated activity during the test. It should not be assumed 
to be a cheap substitute for a properly structured partner model.
4. Track B — VARA licensing for scale
4.1 Applicant entity and process
VARA regulates virtual-asset activity in Dubai outside DIFC. A new application must be made through the 
Dubai Department of Economy and Tourism or a Dubai free zone excluding DIFC. Blockmediary therefore 
needs a separate VARA-eligible legal entity or group company outside DIFC. VARA prefers an LLC or free-
zone company and requires physical presence in Dubai. [9][10]

4.5 CBUAE payment-token overlay
VARA authorisation does not automatically resolve Central Bank requirements. The CBUAE Payment Token 
Services Regulation restricts the provision of payment-token services in or directed to the UAE and restricts 
merchants’ acceptance of virtual assets for goods and services, while expressly excluding Financial Free 
Zones from Article 2’s definition of “the UAE.” A locally licensed VASP may apply for a non-objection 
registration for certain foreign-payment-token custody and transfer activity. [18][19][20]
Blockmediary-specific legal gate
Before any live UAE launch, obtain a written opinion on whether a UAE buyer may prefund USDC or EURC 
into this escrow for settlement of a physical-goods purchase, and whether the proposed partner or VARA 
entity requires a CBUAE licence, registration or non-objection. The answer may differ between an in-DIFC 
pilot, a mainland customer and a cross-border transaction directed to a UAE person.

9. Conclusion
The recommended strategy begins both routes at inception. The DIFC track provides a controlled route to 
customer validation through an authorised partner, while the VARA track builds Blockmediary’s own 
platform for wider Dubai scale. The two tracks remain legally and operationally separated: the partner is 
responsible for the regulated pilot functions, and the VARA applicant is established outside DIFC.
For the academic financial model, Month 18 is the strongest base-case full-licence assumption. Month 12 
remains a credible early case only with fast perimeter resolution, early entity and personnel readiness, a 
substantially complete application within the first half-year, and limited regulatory remediation. Month 24 
provides a reasonable delayed case.
The report’s central caveat is that the exact VARA activity set and CBUAE treatment are not yet settled. 
Transfer and Settlement appears most closely aligned; Custody depends on actual control; Broker-Dealer is 
not demonstrated by the current physical-goods business model. The financial model represents this 
uncertainty through clear scenarios rather than one apparently precise cost figure.
